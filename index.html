Boring, nobody watching

No interest, no novelty, no urgency, nobody expecting it. Four fuels missing. Willpower doesn't beat that, and it never has.

Pick one, now

  • Tell someone a specific time today.
  • Book a short call to report done or not done.
  • Work next to someone, in person or on a call.
  • Delegate it. The stall is the tell.
  • Attach money or a visible consequence.

Every fix that has ever worked for you was external. Every internal one failed. That's an instruction, not a flaw.

Exposing

Risk is fine when it's low or repairable. The thing you avoid is being resented by someone you can't argue back to. Narrower than fear of rejection, and much older.

Do this now

Send three. One sitting, no gaps to reconsider. Score yourself on sent, not on replies.

The ladder, easiest first

  1. Warm messages to lapsed clients
  2. Upsell someone you already work with
  3. Cold outreach
  4. Raise a rate
  5. Events

You won't promise a fantasy result because you know what you can deliver. That's the credibility. Claim more, don't bluff more.

Late, hoping it slides

Going undetected feels safer than doing well. It's an old habit and it's why finishing early never happens.

Do this now, in this order

  1. Send the new time. Before you start. No apology paragraph.
  2. Then start. Badly is fine.

Being chased costs more than being late.

If you're auditing the guilt

Say it as an action, not a character. "I'm a day late on X." Then ask what you'd think of a supplier you like who did the same. That answer is the fair one, and it applies to you too.

A call is eating today

The call is an hour. The dread before and the relief after take the rest. That's the real cost, and the tasks aren't causing it.

Do this now

  1. Write the three things you need from it. Thirty seconds.
  2. Put the boring task in the slot right before the call.

The window before a call is your best focus of the day, because the urgency is already in the room. Stop spending it on rehearsal.

Too late, can't move

You don't get sleepy in front of the TV. You get sleepy after you stand up. It's a physical sequence, not a decision, which is why alarms have never worked.

Do this now

Stand up and walk to the bathroom. Not "go to bed". The tiredness turns up about forty seconds later.

Fix it when you're not tired

Plug timer on the TV, or set the sleep timer at dinner rather than at midnight.

Set it up

Three checks

  • Music, not video.
  • One thing that makes it new, or one visible unlock.
  • Someone to show it to at the end.

When you build something you like, you work for months, you're proud of it, and you show it off. Interest, novelty, progression, recognition, no threat. That engine exists and it's the one to copy.

Nobody left to annoy. You can be the loud one at work now.

Fuel

Name the task. Which of these is present? If none, that's your answer.

InterestCovers a minority of your work and always will. Don't wait for it.
NoveltyDecays by design. When a system goes flat, swap it. That's decay, not failure.
ProgressionFailure states repel you. Unlocks hold you for hours. Break it into levels.
UrgencyYour default. Works every time, costs you the week. Use it early and on purpose.
PeopleStrongest and most underused. A face to report to, a booked call, money attached.
ChemistryCoffee and meds work and make you tense. Tool, not default.

Most people around you are kind and won't push, so they don't hold you to anything. If you want that fuel, you have to build the consequence in.

Corrections

Old charges on the left. What's actually going on, on the right.

"Lazy"

Your attention has always allocated itself this way. It was there before you had any work and it survived punishment, so punishment was never the lever.

"Selfish"

You default to shouldering things. Selfishness is something you had to add deliberately, in adulthood, because you had too little of it.

"Get a job"

You built a life with no authority figure standing over you. That was the point when you were young and it's still the point.

"Not a natural salesman"

Mostly irrelevant. Knowing what you can honestly deliver is what makes a technical person believable. The gap is under-claiming, not under-bluffing.

"My behaviour lags my thinking"

Self-blame widens that gap. Treating a miss as wiring rather than character predicts better follow-through.

"I should manage without a trigger"

You almost never have. Design around it instead of arguing with it.

Evidence

Not a list to read. Six questions to answer, out loud, about right now.

What did you finish recently that you'd happily show someone?
What's working now that wasn't working a year ago?
Who has said something good to you lately, and what were the actual words?
Where has crunch produced work you're proud of?
What did you build for free, for fun, before anyone paid you?
What would someone who likes you say you're underrating?

This page will feel awkward. Self-criticism makes self-compassion feel worst exactly when it works best. Answer them anyway.

Conditions

  • Stand up before you're tired.
  • Yesterday's gym is today's good morning. It needs a slot, not a mood.
  • Protein in the morning, not just coffee.
  • Batch the calls.
  • Music during focus. Video after.
  • Delegate below your pay grade before it becomes a stall.

No tracking, no streak, nothing to break. A second thing to ignore would just be more evidence against you.
